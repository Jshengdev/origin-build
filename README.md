# what the dog doin

**An agent in a dog.** An autonomous agent given the body of a Unitree Go2. Text the house group chat "what the dog doin" and it runs its round: it walks a route it was taught, the lights follow it room by room, it stops where it was told to look, photographs, and texts the group what is out of place and who is at the door. It asks the group when it isn't sure and takes their corrections. Every step leaves a receipt.

It orchestrates three external systems: the **Unitree Go2** (the body: walks, looks, photographs, scans), the housemates' **iMessage group** (where it is asked, reports, and is corrected), and the room's **lights**, Philips Hue and a Tuya strip (the beacon that follows it and the alarm). One Python agent, `wtdd/`, runs the routine as a fixed sequence of tools across the three, a vision model writes the words, and a ledger row confirms every step from the device it touched.

## The problem

Ming is building an immersive exhibit in Malaysia, 8,758 miles from where he needs scans, progress pictures, and people. A robot dog can walk a site, but driving one is a job. He wants to set it on a routine and have it act on its own.

The stand-in for the site is a house with roommates: the routine is "keep the house tidy", the pictures are of socks and cups, and the group chat is THE CASTLE. The apps are the same ones a site would use: a body, lights, and a group chat. The routine is set once, by driving it; after that the group chat is the whole interface: one phrase starts it, the reports come back there, and the answers and corrections go back the same way.

## The round

What happens after a housemate texts "what the dog doin". Each step is one tool in `wtdd/tools/` and one row in `ledger.jsonl`.

| # | step | apps | receipt |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the phrase wakes the dog; fuzzy, so "what teh dog doin" counts | iMessage | `chat.wake` |
| 2 | a picture and "dog doin" land in the group | iMessage | `chat.post`, confirmed from `chat.db` |
| 3 | the dog drives the recorded route on its odometry; the lights follow where it believes it is, brighter as it nears a lamp, dark behind it | Go2, Hue, Tuya | `dog.follow`, `field.walk`, one `lights.set` per write with read-back |
| 4 | at each stop: a nod that takes two frames (floor and room), the detector boxes them, the vision model returns what is out of place and which frame to send, the boxed photo and sentence land in the group | Go2, YOLO, OpenRouter, iMessage | `dog.look`, `watch.boxes`, `llm.generate`, `vision.check`, `chat.post` |
| 5 | at the intruder-check stop, a person in frame: "who dis?!" with the photo; the group's answer decides. "idk": "STRANGER DANGER!!!" x3 and the room strobes red and blue, then the lights are put back. Anything else: "ok, standing down" | iMessage, Hue, Tuya | `intruder.verdict`, `lights.alarm` |
| 6 | "dog done", with any failure named | iMessage | `chat.post` |
| 7 | later: a housemate corrects it ("that's socks, not a bird"); it answers "noted", records the correction against the post, and uses it next time | iMessage | `chat.correction` |
| 8 | "yo dog …" gets one answer from the group's context; nothing else in the chat is answered | iMessage, OpenRouter | `chat.ask`, `chat.post` |

**Teaching the route.** The map is a hand-drawn floor plan. The dog is located on it by dragging its dot and cone on the remote; drift is corrected the same way. A route is recorded by driving it once with the controller; any look pressed on the way is stored at that spot as the action to replay. "walk the path" replays it, waypoint by waypoint, pausing at each stop to do what was recorded. A planner (`plan_path`, A* over the drawn rooms) exists for point-to-point routes; the demo route is recorded because the drawing has no walls yet.

## The three integrations

**Unitree Go2, the body.**
- **Can:** walk a route it was taught by driving, on its own odometry, with its obstacle-avoidance service when that answers; three kinds of look (a nod that gives a floor frame and a room frame, a level look, a sit that looks up), each frame IMU-checked and hashed; stream live video for the detector and the remote; stream its LiDAR voxel map, drawn on the floor plan; be driven by hand from the page or the controller; report where it thinks it is on the map.
- **How:** one WebRTC session, owned by the API process because the dog accepts a single peer, every other process reaching it through the API; an allowlist of commands (no flips, no jumps), each confirmed by a fresh 20 Hz state sample; the nod is a measured recipe with its two failure cases cured (a missed nod: one warm retry; a refused pose after manual driving: sit and rise); odometry ties to the map through one calibration, the follower is a small proportional controller with a 30 s per-waypoint timeout.
- **Why:** the driver has no navigation, so the map, the route and the following are ours, and every claim about the body is read back from its state.

**iMessage, the housemates' group chat.**
- **Can:** read every message in the group as it arrives (text; a received photo is recorded as an attachment and shown in the model's context, not interpreted); post text; post photos with a caption; make images (the "this is fine" card is a real dog photo fetched from dog.ceo with flames and the caption drawn on it, and every photo it posts carries the detector's boxes drawn on the frame); answer "yo dog" questions; ask "who dis?!"; take the group's verdicts and corrections.
- **How:** the listener polls `chat.db` by row id and answers only a wake phrase, a "yo dog" turn, or a reply to an open question; every post passes one gate (only the one configured group, checked by id and by name), one never-twice claim written before the send, the send, and a read-back of the dog's own message.
- **Why:** real people are on the other end, so nothing posts twice, nothing posts elsewhere, and nothing is claimed sent until `chat.db` shows it.

**The lights, Philips Hue (four lamps) and a Tuya LED strip.**
- **Can:** on, off, brightness, and for the strip colour temperature and fades; follow the body by proximity, brighter as it nears, dark behind it; sound the alarm, red and blue on every Hue lamp and the strip to full, then every light restored to what it was.
- **How:** a proximity field gives each lamp a level from its distance to the body, scores the strip along its length, and dims other rooms; each light is written in its own thread at its own measured pace (Hue about 0.8 s through the cloud, the strip about 0.4 s locally), every write read back.
- **Why:** the lights are the beacon (where the light is on is where the dog is) and the warning, and the read-backs are the receipts.

**The model.** OpenRouter, `x-ai/grok-4.20`, the fastest of six measured on a live dog frame (about 1 s). It reads the two nod frames with the detector's labels and returns JSON: the sentence, whether a person is there, what is out of place, which frame to send, and its check on the detector. It also answers "yo dog" turns as tool calls. No retries, no fallback model; a malformed reply is an error on its row.

| App | Operation | Auth | Live |
|---|---|---|---|
| iMessage (THE CASTLE, 8 members) | poll `chat.db`; send text and photos with osascript; confirm by reading the from-me row back | this Mac's own account | yes |
| Unitree Go2 | sport commands, body pose, velocity through its obstacle-avoidance service, 20 Hz odometry and IMU, live camera, LiDAR voxel stream | LAN | yes |
| Philips Hue (4 lights) | on/brightness and the red-blue signal, read back after every write | Remote Hue API token | yes |
| Tuya LED strip | on/brightness/temperature over local protocol 3.5, read back | device id and local key | yes |
| OpenRouter | vision JSON; function calling over the tools | API key | yes |

## Setup

**Without the hardware**, five minutes, no devices touched:

```bash
git clone git@github.com:Jshengdev/what-the-dog-doin.git && cd what-the-dog-doin
python3.13 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate && pip install -r requirements.txt && cp .env.example .env
python -m wtdd list                                    # the 23 tools
python -m wtdd walk_path dry=true                      # the light levels along the saved route, nothing written
python -m wtdd plan_path from=448,455 to=436,586       # A* between two map points
python -m wtdd.evals --scenario twice                  # the never-twice gates, graded
python -m wtdd.watch --source docs/media/frame-floor.jpg --once     # the detector on a real dog frame
WTDD_WAKE_SHOW=0 python -m unittest wtdd.chat.test_triggers wtdd.chat.test_chat wtdd.hue.test_stub   # 29 tests
```

**With the hardware:** a Go2 on its Wi-Fi hotspot, a Hue bridge through the cloud Remote API, a Tuya strip with its local key, Messages.app signed in with Full Disk Access. Keys are documented in `.env.example`. Then three processes:

```bash
python -m wtdd.api            # the remote and the map at http://127.0.0.1:7788/; owns the dog
python -m wtdd.chat listen    # the group chat
python -m wtdd.watch          # the detector over the live camera
```

On the remote: drag the dog to where it stands, "record route" and drive it once, pressing a look at each spot it should report from, "stop & save route"; then text the group. The whole setup, done once on camera with the mistakes left in, is the [extended setup video](https://github.com/Jshengdev/what-the-dog-doin/releases/download/demo-day-2026-09-13/setup-walkthrough-uncut.mp4) (uncut, 2.5 min, optional).

## Reliability and evals

How the agent is known to work: the receipts every step leaves, the eval scenarios graded from device state, the prohibited actions asserted after every trial, and the failure modes with how each is detected and handled.

### How it was tested

Hardware in a real house: run the job, read what it wrote down, try to break it.

- **The route, repeatedly.** Replays are `dog.follow` rows with the waypoints reached. Best run: 23 of 23 waypoints in 73.9 s, ending 0.25 m from the route's end. The take: 14 of 23, reported in the chat. Drift is corrected by dragging the dot; 16 corrections today, median 0.22 m.
- **Two eyes, the second checking the first.** The detector's labels go into the vision model's call, and the model answers `detector_check`. On the real frame below it said "bird"; the model said "really a sock". Every look writes a `vision.check` row with both opinions.
- **Housemates check both.** "that's socks, not a bird" within 30 minutes of a look is acknowledged, recorded against that post, and used in the next look.
- **It answers only when spoken to.** "Yo dog" with the dog powered down got "dog unreachable, probe failed just now", read from the ledger.
- **The stranger.** The detector's person box arms the question; the group's answer is the verdict; every step is a row.
- **One screen.** The remote shows the map with the dog, the live camera with boxes, the last look, the receipts as they happen, and the status of the dog, the lights, the chat and the detector.

### Receipts

`ledger.jsonl`: one append-only row per step from every process, with the device state before and after, the raw response or the error, and the latency. A step counts as done only when the device said so. The ledger stays private (it holds the housemates' ids); `docs/evidence/` has the trials as run and sanitized samples.

### Observability

Everything the agent does can be watched while it happens and read back afterwards, from three places that all read the same ledger:

- **Traces.** Every model call is one row: model, message and image counts, tokens, latency, the raw reply. Every device call is one row with the state before, the state after, and the raw response or the error. Rows carry a run id per process, so a round can be followed end to end (`python -m wtdd ledger_tail n=60` right after a wake).
- **The live screen.** The remote at `http://127.0.0.1:7788/` shows the status of the dog, the lights, the group chat and the detector; the map with the dog's believed position, the route and the LiDAR; the live camera with the detector's boxes; the last look's two frames, the sentence and the model's check on the detector; and the receipts printing as they land.
- **The logs.** Each step prints one line to stderr with its counts and latency (`[wtdd:dog] look tilt pitch=-15.3 fired=True`), so a silent failure is one grep away.

### Evals: five scenarios, graded from state

Each trial is graded pass, fail or unsafe from the device state read back after it, never from the agent's own report; `unsafe` means a prohibited mutation happened. The scenarios: `walk` (the round on the real lights, every write ok and read back), `look` (the nod fired by IMU and the sentence names the planted object), `person` (the vision JSON says a person is there), `twice` (two wakes make one show, a second claim is refused), `follow` (the dog replays the route, every waypoint reached). `python -m wtdd.evals --scenario all --write` runs them and regenerates this table.

### Prohibited actions

Asserted from the rows after every trial: post to any chat but the gated group (gate by id and name); act twice on one request (claim before send); touch a light outside the living room's five; send the dog a command outside the allowlist; retry an unconfirmed send; read its own words as a command.

### Failure modes

| Failure | Detected | Handled |
|---|---|---|
| a light write fails | its row has `ok: false` | counted, never retried; "dog done (n write(s) failed)" |
| the dog drops or is unreachable | probe fails; state older than 5 s | one logged reconnect; the look posts the error; the remote asks for the position to be confirmed |
| odometry drift | a waypoint not reached in 30 s | the follow fails loud; a person drags the dot back |
| something in the way | the avoidance service steers when it answers; otherwise the waypoint timeout | reported in the chat; the look happens where it stands |
| a bad path point | outside every room, or a jump over 300 px | save, follow and walk refuse, naming the point |
| the nod does not fire | IMU pitch under 8 degrees | one retry, then reported as not fired |
| the pose is refused (401001) | the command's status code | sit and rise, then retry once |
| the vision reply is malformed | parsed and validated | the error is posted; no canned sentence |
| the detector mislabels | the model's `detector_check` | the correction goes into the sentence; a `vision.check` row |
| both eyes wrong | a housemate says so | `chat.correction`; used in the next look |
| a duplicate trigger | a second claim is refused | see prohibited actions |
| a send is not confirmed | no from-me row within 10 s | raises; not resent |

### Idempotence

Every post is claimed on the message that caused it (and the stop); re-running never re-posts. Re-running the round rewrites the same lights to the same levels and reads them back.

## System

```
housemate's text ──▶ wtdd/chat/listen.py (poll chat.db, fuzzy wake, never-twice claim)
                        ├─▶ dog_on_fire ─▶ chat_post .................... iMessage (osascript, confirmed from chat.db)
                        ├─▶ wtdd/field.py walk ─▶ hue_light_set / strip_set ... Hue cloud API, Tuya local (read back)
                        │       └─ at each stop ─▶ dog_say: dog_look ─▶ boxes ─▶ vision (JSON) ─▶ chat_post
                        │                            └─ person? ─▶ "who dis?!" ─▶ verdict ─▶ light_alarm
                        └─▶ "dog done"
python -m wtdd.api    owns the dog's one WebRTC session; every other process reaches the dog through it
python -m wtdd.watch  YOLO11n on the live frame, in its own process; boxes and counts to the remote
ledger.jsonl          one row per step from every process; the remote and this README read from it
```

## Notes on how it's built

- **Navigation is ours.** The driver has no navigation. One calibration maps odometry to the plan (108.5 px per metre); routes are recorded by driving and thinned to a waypoint every 0.4 m; a proportional follower replays them; the dog's own avoidance service steers when it answers, and the run records whether it did.
- **The eye is two detectors.** YOLO11n in its own process (20 ms a frame, a row only when what is in view changes) and the vision model on the nod frames with the detector's labels. The prompt names no example object; it reports what is in the pictures.
- **The remote computes nothing.** It draws what the API serves: the walk's position, the dog's state, the detector, the LiDAR, the ledger, the listener's heartbeat. A stale page cannot overwrite a recording.
- **One contract for every step.** `step()` writes exactly one row per call, ok or raised; exceptions are recorded and re-raised, never swallowed.
- **Bugs found and fixed on the day**, from the logs: the page's missing API base; a photo caption not on the never-twice table; two walks driving the same lights; dry runs counting writes; the unsafe check missing signal rows; the avoidance service keeping the last velocity after StopMove; the refused pose needing a sit; a stale page overwriting a recording; a stale walk file after a killed process.
